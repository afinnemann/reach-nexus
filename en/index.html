Why this project

Intensifying extreme weather events disproportionately impact marginalized populations, increasing existing health inequalities and vulnerabilities. This complex "polycrisis" is studied from a systems perspective. Four case studies are developed where experts and stakeholders collaboratively model vulnerabilities and identify feedback loops to find key intervention targets. Data collection follows, using a citizen-science approach to ensure that the models reflect local contexts and lived experiences. These models are refined over time, ultimately generating site-specific policy recommendations and a transferable modelling framework applicable to other vulnerable urban settings worldwide.

Three phases over five years

I

Understand

Foundational analysis: baseline health-system capacity, climate exposure and historic vulnerability across the four sites, viewed through a climate-justice lens.

II

Integrate

Local working groups co-develop models that capture the interactions between urban planning, health policy and climate adaptation, fed by citizen-science data.

III

Improve

Translate findings into living-lab pilots in Brazil and the Netherlands, plus model-based policy advice for partner municipalities.

Project log

  • 2025-12-10

    Project funded under the NWO–FAPESP Merian Fund call

    REACH Nexus has been selected as one of five Brazilian–Dutch consortia funded under the NWO–FAPESP Cooperation Brazil–The Netherlands 2024 call "Extreme Heat and Water Events: Mitigating the Adverse Health Effects of Climate Change in Cities." The five-year programme begins on 1 May 2026 and is jointly led by Reinout Wiers (UvA) and Leandro Giatti (USP).
