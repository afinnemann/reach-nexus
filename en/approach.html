Approach & references

REACH Nexus brings together urban planning, public health, environmental law, clinical psychology, and systems modelling into a single comparative programme. The four cases are not just settings — they are the research design.

Conceptual framing

The project takes a systems perspective on the polycrisis — the entanglement of climate change, urban inequality, and health-system fragility. We use planetary-health concepts in dialogue with emerging "nexus" frameworks, and we work backwards from cascading vulnerabilities to find intervention points that address root causes rather than symptoms.

Methods

  • Causal-loop diagrams and agent-based models (WP2) — site-specific structures, refined iteratively against citizen-science data.
  • Health-system capacity assessment (WP3) — secondary data on climate-sensitive disease, mental-health surveys after extreme events, primary-care assessments.
  • Citizen-science co-research (WP4) — participatory workshops and mobile data-collection tools that capture lived experience during and after extreme weather.
  • Living-lab interventions (WP5) — one Brazilian, one Dutch pilot intervention informed by the comparative model.
