Ethics

The project works with people in vulnerable urban communities, including indigenous residents in Manaus and informal-settlement residents in São Paulo. Ethical conduct is not an add-on; it shapes who decides what is studied, how data are collected, and what is shown publicly.

Ethics review

Empirical work is reviewed by the institutional review boards of the host institutions in each jurisdiction — UvA Faculty of Social and Behavioural Sciences (and Amsterdam UMC where clinical data are involved) in the Netherlands; the Plataforma Brasil and the relevant CEP at USP / Fiocruz Amazônia in Brazil. Approval references will be listed here once issued.

Community-engagement principles

  • Communities are co-researchers, not subjects. The Local Working Groups hold veto over what is studied at their site.
  • Free, prior, and informed consent — collected at the right moments, in the right languages, in accessible formats — is the baseline.
  • Findings are returned to communities in plain language before they are published academically.
  • For Parque das Tribos, the project follows indigenous data-sovereignty principles agreed with the community association and Fiocruz Amazônia.

Public representation of place

Every site page on this website opens with a short "How this place is shown here" block — what is shown, what deliberately is not, and who decided. This is a design pattern, not a policy buried in a back page. It is enforced in the data layer: the site map cannot render a marker without an explicit precision field signed off by the local partner.
