AI use

This page is a transparency note. It explains where the project uses large language models (LLMs) and other AI methods, where it deliberately does not, and how decisions get reviewed.

Where AI is used

  • Evidence discovery and harmonisation in the WP3 disaster-health data pipeline (project name CLIHEALTH). LLMs assist in screening literature, structuring extracted data, and harmonising heterogeneous sources. A human researcher reviews every output before it enters the analysis dataset.
  • Translation drafts between English and Portuguese on this website. All public-facing copy is reviewed by a human translator before publishing.
  • Code assistance in software components, with code reviewed and tested by the team.

Where AI is not used

  • Generating mental-health survey responses or qualitative quotes. All such data come from human participants under informed consent.
  • Decisions affecting individual people in the case communities — diagnosis, intervention assignment, eligibility for participation. These remain human-led.
  • Synthesising community testimony. Where residents describe lived experiences, those words are not paraphrased by an LLM in any public output.

Review and appeal

AI-use questions are reviewed by the Ethics Thematic Committee. To raise a concern about how the project uses AI, contact the consortium via the contact page.
