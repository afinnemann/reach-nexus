# REACH Nexus — Code of Conduct

This code applies to everyone working on or contributing to REACH Nexus — staff, students, partners, advisors, and contractors — across both research activity and the public-facing project assets including this website.

## Our commitments

We work in vulnerable urban communities. We commit to:

- **Free, prior, and informed consent** with every participant, in their first language and in accessible formats.
- **Returning findings to communities in plain language** before they are published academically.
- **Equal voice for the Brazilian and Dutch sides of the consortium** in scientific decisions, authorship, and external representation.
- **A working environment free of harassment, discrimination, or intimidation** for all team members and contributors.
- **Honesty about uncertainty** in our research outputs. We do not present preliminary findings as settled.
- **Transparent acknowledgement** of who did what work and how AI tools (where used) shaped that work.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Any form of harassment, including unwelcome sexual attention, slurs, threats, or intimidation.
- Publishing or speaking about a community in a way that overrides the Local Working Group's veto.
- Sharing identifiable participant data outside the agreed access channels.
- Misrepresenting AI-assisted output as fully human-authored, or vice versa.
- Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern under this code.

## Reporting

Concerns can be raised:

- to either of the two PIs (Reinout Wiers, Leandro Giatti),
- to the consortium ombudsperson (named on the contact page; rotates),
- to the integrity office of the relevant host institution (UvA, USP, FGV, Fiocruz, etc.).

Anyone raising a concern in good faith is protected from retaliation. The Management Committee responds within 10 working days to acknowledge a concern; investigations follow the host institution's research-integrity procedures.

## Scope

This code applies to project work, project events, the public website, and project communications. It complements (not replaces) host institutions' own integrity codes, which take precedence within their jurisdiction.

This code is a living document. Last updated 2026.
